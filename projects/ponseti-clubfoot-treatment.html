Thesis Project

The Ponseti Clubfoot Treatment Simulation is an immersive learning platform developed for CURE International India Trust (CIIT) to train healthcare providers in the Ponseti Method for clubfoot correction. Integrating anatomy education, Pirani assessment, procedural training, hand-tracking interactions, and AI-powered learning, it enables interactive, hands-on practice in Virtual Reality. The platform transforms traditional mentor-dependent training into a scalable, standardized, and engaging learning experience, bridging theoretical knowledge and clinical practice while improving accessibility to specialized healthcare education.

UX Design Principles

The interface follows Don Norman's principles of Human-Centred Design, adapting the system to a clinician's mental model, with Shneiderman's Eight Golden Rules informing layout consistency, error tolerance, and feedback across all 9 modules.

Feedback Loops

Every interaction produces an immediate visual or haptic response, aligned with Norman's action evaluation cycle.

Consistency

UI language, color coding (CAVE stages, Pirani severity), and spatial layout stay uniform across all modules.

Error Recovery

Learners can retry any module without penalty, supporting Shneiderman's principle of forgiving design.

Human Factors and Heuristics

Nielsen's 10 Usability Heuristics were applied in a VR-specific context, supplemented by Fitts' Law for interaction target sizing and Cognitive Load Theory to manage simultaneous clinical decision-making and procedural execution.

Error Prevention Over Correction

Anatomical constraints built into the foot model resist physically implausible manipulation.

Recognition Over Recall

Pirani scoring is presented as a guided visual checklist rather than a blank recall task.

Intrinsic Load Management

Each module isolates one clinical stage, preventing split-attention between simultaneous tasks.

Visibility of System Status

CAVE angle meters and the Pirani scorecard remain persistently visible in the HUD, positioned to match the natural spatial expectations of a clinician leaning over a patient, reducing the need to interrupt the procedure to check progress.

The Clubfoot Museum

An extension of the training simulation built for investors, stakeholders, and partners. Where the simulation trains clinicians, the Museum is an immersive storytelling space highlighting CURE India's program reach and impact through interactive exhibits, including AI-powered conversational avatars of Dr. Mathew Varghese and Dr. Ignacio Ponseti, built with ConvAI and voice cloning, letting visitors ask questions about the treatment method and its global history in natural dialogue.

Reflection

This project connected surprisingly well with my long-standing interest in biology. I genuinely enjoyed diving into medical research and understanding how a treatment method works beyond the textbook level. Professionally, I grew more confident by working in a completely new domain and learning how design skills could create impact in healthcare, and by exploring how AI fits into XR. One of the highlights was becoming a finalist at XRCC and seeing our work recognized by the XR community.
